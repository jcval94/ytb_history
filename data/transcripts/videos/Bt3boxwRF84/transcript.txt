If the sun were to disappear tomorrow, these cave spiders could go on living for thousands of years, completely in the dark. If you go right to the border of Greece and Albania, you head down this dark cave, and then you wade through chest-high water for about 50 meters, you find something really strange, this thick, spongy substance that is everywhere. It spans a hundred square meters, and if you look closely, you'll find that it's teeming with a hundred thousand spiders. This is the world's largest spider web. But what's strange is that this cave is in complete darkness. There is no sun, so no photosynthesis, and no plants, or any other traditional sources of food. So how can these spiders survive? Well, things get even weirder when scientists looked at the kinds of spiders. They found two species, one bigger one and one smaller one. Usually, the bigger one eats the smaller one, but here they were living in harmony. So what was going on? Well, they found the answer to both of these questions when they looked at the walls of the cave, which was covered in this white slime. When they put this slime under the microscope, they realized there were sulfur-eating bacteria there, which were feeding off the high sulfur concentrations in the rock. And cave flies would then eat that slime. And so you got millions of cave flies, which ended up creating this near-infinite buffet for the spiders.
