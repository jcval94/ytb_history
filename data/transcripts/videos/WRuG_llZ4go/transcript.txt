A site more significant than the ruins of Machu Picchu, archaeologists say this Inca citadel in Peru could very well be known as Taqrachullo, which means Mother Rock in the language of the Incas. The complex features 700 structures, mostly made of stone, on a site more than triple the size of Machu Picchu. Peruvian researchers sponsored by the Ministry of Culture began digging at the site, around 140 miles southeast of Machu Picchu, in 2019, according to National Geographic. The archaeologist leading the ongoing research says nearly 3,000 gold, silver and copper sequins have been found here in recent years. Peru is home to hundreds of archaeological sites, including citadels built before and during the Inca Empire.
