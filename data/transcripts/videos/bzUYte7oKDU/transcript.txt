This bricklaying robot has traded in the trowel for cutting-edge innovation. Meet Walter, or the Wall Layering Terabase Robot. It takes on one of the toughest, most repetitive jobs on building sites, helping solve the serious shortage of skilled workers. Dr. Jan Tolensky is the founder and CEO of JT Lifestyle Homes and the robot's co-developer. When I did the survey, the average age of bricklaying in the UK is 46 years, which means, and nobody's learning it. So in 20 years' time, who is going to build the houses if you need a bricklayer? Walter could be the construction industry's much-needed hero. This robot does five bricklayers and one laborer in one hour, operated by one man. It works in all weathers, doesn't need scaffolding. It's got all the plans in its head. It works by itself. All you need to do is supply it with the two pallets of bricks, and it just flies. The tolerance of the wall is two millimeters. To achieve that with a bricklayer is probably only one percent who is that good. The bricklaying robot builds walls at about 108 square feet per hour. It uses grooved, robot-ready bricks which allow it to grip, lift, and place. Walter requires a prepared first layer of bricks and must be monitored by trained workers. It is also designed to be more environmentally friendly. It uses a glue so there is no cement. And as you know, mortar, as you know, cement, is the biggest polluter of earth. Six percent of CO2 comes out of manufacturing cement and using cement on a building site. With the UK government pushing to build 1.5 million new homes, robots like Walter could be game changers. Tolensky says Walter could help attract young people to bricklaying. We've done the research. I promise you, we will not say you'll be a bricklayer. We will say you will be a robot operator building with bricks. They love it. They enjoy it because it's like playing computer games. The future is we can bring in the young people to work on a building site because they prefer working rather with robots than doing the manual work.
