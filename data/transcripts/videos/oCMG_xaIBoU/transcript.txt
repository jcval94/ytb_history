Is this the way? Will hail a ride in the sky? Chinese company AutoFlight has developed one of the world's largest eVTOL, an electric vertical takeoff and landing vehicle. The five-ton aircraft, named the Matrix, can carry up to 10 passengers. The prototype has a wingspan of 20 meters and features a multitude of rotors and propellers. AutoFlight says it can travel for an hour without charging. When it will enter production or operation is still uncertain, though. And the competition is also ready to spread its wings. U.S. company Joby's flagship air taxi is in the final stages of certification by the U.S. aviation regulator, and it's expected to carry its first passengers in Dubai in 2026. Archer hopes to obtain certification for its Midnight aircraft by 2028 and become the official air taxi provider for the Los Angeles Olympics. UK-based company Vertical Aerospace is also eyeing certification for its Velo aircraft in 2028. It may take some time before vehicles like these can pick up passengers. Not only because of safety regulations, but also because the necessary infrastructure, insurance and other essentials still need to be built. It will take at least another three years to see something more viable, and for any big rollout of a full scale, I would say probably five to 10 years. So there's still a long way to go before we see sky taxis swirling above our heads.
